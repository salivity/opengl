How OpenGL Transforms World to View Coordinates

In OpenGL, transforming vertices from world coordinates to view coordinates—often called camera space or eye space—is accomplished through matrix multiplication using a view matrix. This transformation shifts and rotates the entire 3D scene so that the virtual camera sits at the origin \((0, 0, 0)\) looking down the negative Z-axis, with the Y-axis pointing upward and the X-axis pointing to the right. By applying the inverse of the camera's position and orientation to every vertex in the world, OpenGL translates the global environment into a local coordinate system relative to the observer.

The Purpose of View Space

World coordinates define where objects reside in an absolute 3D space. However, to render a scene from a specific perspective, the graphics pipeline requires geometry defined relative to the viewer. View coordinates establish this perspective, serving as the intermediate stage between world space and clip space (which is produced after projection).

Constructing the View Matrix

OpenGL does not have a physical camera object. Instead, it simulates a camera by moving the entire world in the opposite direction. If the camera moves forward, the world must move backward; if the camera turns right, the world must rotate left.

To build the view matrix, three vectors are typically required:

  1. Camera Position (\(P\)): The location of the camera in world space.
  2. Camera Target (\(T\)): The point in world space the camera is looking at.
  3. Up Vector (\(U_{world}\)): A vector defining which direction is "up" in the world (usually \((0, 1, 0)\)).

From these vectors, a new orthogonal coordinate frame is calculated:

  • Direction Vector (\(D\)): Points from the target to the camera position (the positive Z-axis in camera space):
    \[D = \text{normalize}(P - T)\]
  • Right Vector (\(R\)): Perpendicular to both the up vector and the direction vector (the positive X-axis in camera space):
    \[R = \text{normalize}(U_{world} \times D)\]
  • True Up Vector (\(U\)): Perpendicular to both the direction and right vectors (the positive Y-axis in camera space):
    \[U = D \times R\]

The Mathematics: Rotation and Translation

The complete view matrix (\(V\)) is the combination of two distinct transformations: translating the camera to the origin and rotating the scene to align with the camera’s orientation.

The translation matrix (\(T_{view}\)) negates the camera's position:

\[T_{view} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & -P_x \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & -P_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -P_z \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

The rotation matrix (\(R_{view}\)) aligns the world axes with the camera axes using the transpose of the camera's orientation matrix:

\[R_{view} = \begin{bmatrix} R_x & R_y & R_z & 0 \\ U_x & U_y & U_z & 0 \\ D_x & D_y & D_z & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

Multiplying these two matrices yields the standard LookAt view matrix:

\[V = R_{view} \times T_{view} = \begin{bmatrix} R_x & R_y & R_z & -(R \cdot P) \\ U_x & U_y & U_z & -(U \cdot P) \\ D_x & D_y & D_z & -(D \cdot P) \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

Execution in the Vertex Shader

In modern OpenGL, this transformation occurs on the GPU inside the vertex shader. The application constructs the view matrix on the CPU (often using math libraries like GLM) and passes it to the shader as a uniform matrix.

The transformation of a world-space vertex position is executed through standard matrix-vector multiplication:

#version 330 core
layout (location = 0) in vec3 aPos;

uniform mat4 model;
uniform mat4 view;
uniform mat4 projection;

void main()
{
    // Step 1: Object to World Space
    vec4 worldPosition = model * vec4(aPos, 1.0);

    // Step 2: World to View Space
    vec4 viewPosition = view * worldPosition;

    // Step 3: View to Clip Space
    gl_Position = projection * viewPosition;
}

Once multiplied by the view matrix, the vertex coordinates reflect their exact distance and angle relative to the virtual camera, preparing them for the projection matrix to apply perspective or orthographic division.
