Texture Footprint Inquiry for Pixel Coverage in OpenGL

Texture footprint inquiry in OpenGL allows fragment shaders to inspect the exact region of texels a pixel projection covers on a surface, rather than simply returning a filtered color sample. Primarily introduced through extensions such as GL_NV_shader_texture_footprint, this technique evaluates the footprint by calculating screen-space derivatives, determining the appropriate level of detail (LoD), and returning a geometric representation of the texel region mapped to the pixel. This article explains the underlying mechanism of how these extensions assess and quantify pixel surface coverage in texture space.

Derivative Calculation and Projection Analysis

During standard rasterization, a pixel is not a dimensionless point; it projects onto the textured primitive as an area or footprint, often resembling an ellipse or a parallelogram depending on the view angle. The GPU determines this area by evaluating screen-space derivatives of texture coordinates (\(\frac{\partial uv}{\partial x}\) and \(\frac{\partial uv}{\partial y}\)) across neighboring pixels in a 2x2 fragment quad. These gradients indicate the rate of change of texture coordinates relative to screen coordinates, defining the dimensions and distortion of the projected surface area on the texture map.

Level of Detail (LoD) Selection

Once the derivatives define the shape and size of the projected footprint, the hardware maps this area to one or two mipmap levels. Standard texture filtering selects an LoD where the pixel covers roughly one to four texels. Texture footprint inquiry extensions repurpose this calculation to categorize the surface coverage. The extension selects the base mip level that best encompasses the major axis of the projected footprint while maintaining manageable sampling bounds, ensuring that the evaluated texel region accurately reflects the geometric resolution required for the surface.

Texel Grid Quantization and Footprint Masks

Instead of blending the texels within the calculated footprint into a single color, footprint inquiry functions (such as textureFootprintNV) output structured data describing the physical footprint. The GPU quantizes the area into a localized grid of coarse texel blocks (typically organized as \(2 \times 2\) or \(4 \times 4\) sub-regions at the chosen LoD).

The query returns three critical pieces of data regarding the coverage area:

  1. Anchor Coordinates: The base coordinate identifying the origin of the bounding region in the texture's integer texel space.
  2. LoD Index: The mipmap level chosen to represent the footprint.
  3. Coverage Bitmask: A boolean or bit-packed mask indicating which specific sub-tiles within the bounding footprint are actually touched by the projected pixel surface.

Evaluating Coverage and Boundary Conditions

By checking the coverage mask, the shader precisely determines how much of the pixel's surface area overlaps individual texels or regions of the texture. If the projected pixel spans a wide surface area due to steep viewing angles (anisotropy), the footprint expands, setting more bits in the coverage mask or shifting to a coarser LoD. If the pixel covers a minimal texture area, the footprint contracts to fewer active sub-tiles at a higher-resolution mip level.

This geometric evaluation enables applications to assess surface coverage without executing costly memory fetches. It serves critical roles in partially resident (sparse) textures by verifying data residency before sampling, driving real-time texture streaming systems based on actual surface demand, and implementing custom multi-rate shading or anisotropic reconstruction algorithms.
