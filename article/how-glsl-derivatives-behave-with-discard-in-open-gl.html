How GLSL Derivatives Behave With Discard in OpenGL

This article explains how OpenGL handles GLSL derivative functions—such as dFdx, dFdy, and implicit derivatives used in texture sampling—when fragments within a quad are terminated using the discard keyword. Modern GPUs evaluate fragment shaders in parallel 2x2 pixel quads to compute these derivatives using finite differences between neighboring pixels. When a fragment executes a discard statement, this quad structure is disrupted, leading to undefined results, visual artifacts, or reliance on hardware-specific helper invocations depending on where the derivative is sampled relative to the discard operation.

The Mechanics of 2x2 Fragment Quads

Graphics hardware processes fragment shaders in groups of four pixels arranged in a 2x2 grid, known as a quad. GLSL derivative functions (dFdx, dFdy, and fwidth) calculate the rate of change of a variable across screen space by subtracting the values computed in adjacent fragments:

  • Horizontal derivative (dFdx): The difference between the left and right fragments in the quad.
  • Vertical derivative (dFdy): The difference between the top and bottom fragments in the quad.

Texture sampling functions that use automatic Level of Detail (LOD) selection, such as texture(), rely internally on these same derivatives to determine the correct mipmap level.

Discard and Helper Invocations

When a fragment invokes discard, it marks that pixel so it will not write depth, stencil, or color data to the framebuffer. However, the underlying hardware thread cannot always terminate immediately if other fragments in the 2x2 quad remain active.

To keep derivative math functional for surviving neighbors, most GPU architectures convert discarded fragments into "helper invocations." A helper invocation continues executing instructions in lockstep with active fragments to provide the necessary data for finite difference calculations, but its outputs are ultimately thrown away before rasterization.

Derivatives Evaluated Before discard

If derivative calculations or standard texture lookups are performed before the conditional branch leading to discard, the behavior is well-defined:

  • All four fragments in the quad possess valid data at that point in execution.
  • Finite differences can be accurately calculated.
  • Subsequent discards do not retroactively corrupt earlier calculations.

Derivatives Evaluated After discard

Evaluating derivatives after a discard statement creates undefined behavior according to the OpenGL and GLSL specifications:

  1. Divergent Execution: Once discard is triggered conditionally, the fragments within the quad diverge. Under the GLSL specification, derivative calculations executed within non-uniform flow control yield undefined values.
  2. Stale or Uninitialized Data: While hardware may keep a discarded fragment running as a helper invocation, compilers frequently optimize discarded paths by omitting code execution for dead lanes. If a discarded fragment skips variable updates that occur after the discard call, neighboring active fragments will compute differences against stale or uninitialized registers.
  3. Mipmapping Artifacts: When texture() is called after a discard, the corrupted derivatives produce sudden spikes in the calculated LOD. This commonly manifests as visible seams, black lines, or abrupt transitions to the lowest-resolution mipmap along the boundary where fragments were discarded.

Best Practices to Avoid Undefined Derivatives

To guarantee consistent behavior across different GPU vendors and driver implementations when using discard:

  • Place Derivatives First: Compute all screen-space derivatives, or perform texture reads that rely on implicit derivatives, before any conditional discard logic in the shader.
  • Use Explicit LOD Sampling: If a texture must be sampled after a discard, replace implicit sampling functions with explicit alternatives such as textureLod() or textureGrad(). The textureGrad() function allows you to pass precomputed derivatives from an earlier stage in the shader where the quad was still intact.
