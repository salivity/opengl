How OpenGL Clips Primitives Against View Frustum

This article provides a technical overview of how OpenGL clips rendering primitives against the view frustum volume. In the graphics pipeline, clipping ensures that only geometry within the visible field of view is processed for rasterization. This process occurs in homogeneous clip space immediately following vertex shading, where primitives are evaluated against six boundary planes, subdivided or discarded as necessary, and interpolated to maintain perspective-correct vertex attributes before being mapped to normalized device coordinates.

The Clipping Stage in the Pipeline

Clipping takes place after vertex processing (including optional tessellation and geometry shading) and primitive assembly, but strictly before perspective division. The vertex shader outputs positions in 4D homogeneous clip coordinates:

\[(x_c, y_c, z_c, w_c)\]

Performing clipping before the perspective divide avoids mathematical singularities caused by dividing by zero or negative \(w_c\) values for geometry situated at or behind the camera plane.

Frustum Boundaries and Clipping Equations

The standard OpenGL viewing frustum is defined by six clipping planes representing the left, right, bottom, top, near, and far boundaries. A vertex is inside the viewing volume if it satisfies the following inequalities:

  • \(-w_c \le x_c \le w_c\) (Left and Right)
  • \(-w_c \le y_c \le w_c\) (Bottom and Top)
  • \(-w_c \le z_c \le w_c\) (Near and Far in standard OpenGL)

If the extension GL_ARB_clip_control (or OpenGL 4.5+) is used with a zero-to-one depth range, the near-to-far condition adjusts to \(0 \le z_c \le w_c\).

Trivial Rejection and Acceptance

Before executing costly geometric clipping algorithms, OpenGL performs bounding tests:

  1. Trivial Acceptance: If all vertices of a primitive satisfy the boundary conditions across all six planes, the primitive passes through unaltered.
  2. Trivial Rejection: If all vertices of a primitive lie on the outside of any single boundary plane, the entire primitive is culled immediately, requiring no further computation.

Primitives that are neither trivially accepted nor trivially rejected straddle the frustum boundary and must be clipped.

Primitive-Specific Clipping Mechanisms

The implementation handles geometry based on the primitive assembly type:

  • Points: Points are treated as atomic. If the point's coordinates lie outside the clipping volume, it is completely discarded. Points are never subdivided.
  • Lines: Line segments are clipped against each plane using parametric line-clipping algorithms (such as the Liang-Barsky or Cohen-Sutherland algorithms adapted to homogeneous space). The intersection parameter \(t \in [0, 1]\) is computed for the intersection with the plane, and the exterior endpoint is replaced with the newly calculated intersection point.
  • Triangles: Polygons are clipped plane-by-plane, typically utilizing the Sutherland-Hodgman polygon clipping algorithm. When a triangle is intersected by a plane, the portion outside the plane is cut away. Depending on the orientation:
    • If one vertex is inside and two are outside, the algorithm produces a smaller single triangle.
    • If two vertices are inside and one is outside, the resulting quadrilateral is retriangulated into two distinct triangles before continuing through the pipeline.

Attribute Interpolation

When new vertices are generated along the clipping planes, their associated attributes (such as texture coordinates, normals, vertex colors, and generic fragment inputs) must be calculated.

Because clipping is performed in homogeneous clip space, attributes are linearly interpolated along the edge using the computed parametric intersection factor \(t\):

\[\text{Attribute}_{\text{new}} = (1 - t) \cdot \text{Attribute}_1 + t \cdot \text{Attribute}_2\]

Interpolating in 4D space prior to perspective division guarantees that perspective correctness is preserved for all generated vertices once perspective division is applied later.

Transition to Normalized Device Coordinates

Once all primitives are clipped to the boundaries of the frustum, the hardware performs the perspective divide by dividing \(x_c\), \(y_c\), and \(z_c\) by \(w_c\). This converts the surviving and newly generated coordinates into Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC), where all visible geometry resides cleanly inside an axis-aligned cube with coordinates spanning \([-1, 1]\) along each axis. The primitives are then handed off to the viewport transformation and rasterization stages.
