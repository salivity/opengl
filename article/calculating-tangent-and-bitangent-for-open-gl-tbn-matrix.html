Calculating Tangent and Bitangent for OpenGL TBN Matrix

Normal mapping in OpenGL requires transforming tangent-space normal vectors into world or view space using a Tangent-Bitangent-Normal (TBN) matrix. This article explains the geometric theory and algebraic steps required to calculate tangent and bitangent vectors from a 3D triangle's vertices and UV coordinates. It details the inversion of the coordinate mapping matrix, per-vertex accumulation, Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, and the assembly of the final TBN matrix inside the graphics pipeline.

The Relationship Between 3D Space and UV Space

A surface triangle is defined by three 3D positions (\(P_1, P_2, P_3\)) and three corresponding 2D texture coordinates (\(UV_1, UV_2, UV_3\)).

Two edge vectors describe the geometry of the triangle in 3D space:

  • \(E_1 = P_2 - P_1\)
  • \(E_2 = P_3 - P_1\)

Similarly, the changes in texture coordinates along the same edges are:

  • \(\Delta U_1 = U_2 - U_1, \quad \Delta V_1 = V_2 - V_1\)
  • \(\Delta U_2 = U_3 - U_1, \quad \Delta V_2 = V_3 - V_1\)

Because the edge vectors \(E_1\) and \(E_2\) lie on the triangle plane, they can be expressed as a linear combination of the Tangent vector (\(T\), parallel to the \(U\) texture axis) and the Bitangent vector (\(B\), parallel to the \(V\) texture axis):

\[E_1 = \Delta U_1 \cdot T + \Delta V_1 \cdot B\] \[E_2 = \Delta U_2 \cdot T + \Delta V_2 \cdot B\]

Matrix Formulation and Inversion

The equations above can be represented in matrix form:

\[\begin{bmatrix} E_{1x} & E_{1y} & E_{1z} \\ E_{2x} & E_{2y} & E_{2z} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta U_1 & \Delta V_1 \\ \Delta U_2 & \Delta V_2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} T_x & T_y & T_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{bmatrix}\]

To solve for the matrix containing \(T\) and \(B\), multiply both sides by the inverse of the \(\Delta UV\) matrix:

\[\begin{bmatrix} T_x & T_y & T_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta U_1 & \Delta V_1 \\ \Delta U_2 & \Delta V_2 \end{bmatrix}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} E_{1x} & E_{1y} & E_{1z} \\ E_{2x} & E_{2y} & E_{2z} \end{bmatrix}\]

The inverse of a \(2 \times 2\) matrix is defined as:

\[\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad - bc} \begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix}\]

Applying this formula yields:

\[\begin{bmatrix} T \\ B \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{\Delta U_1 \Delta V_2 - \Delta U_2 \Delta V_1} \begin{bmatrix} \Delta V_2 & -\Delta V_1 \\ -\Delta U_2 & \Delta U_1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} E_1 \\ E_2 \end{bmatrix}\]

Expanding the components:

\[T = \frac{1}{f} (\Delta V_2 \cdot E_1 - \Delta V_1 \cdot E_2)\] \[B = \frac{1}{f} (-\Delta U_2 \cdot E_1 + \Delta U_1 \cdot E_2)\]

where \(f = \Delta U_1 \Delta V_2 - \Delta U_2 \Delta V_1\).

Implementation Algorithm

The algorithmic process for an arbitrary mesh involves three steps:

1. Calculate Triangle Vectors

For every triangle, compute edges and UV deltas:

glm::vec3 edge1 = pos2 - pos1;
glm::vec3 edge2 = pos3 - pos1;
glm::vec2 deltaUV1 = uv2 - uv1;
glm::vec2 deltaUV2 = uv3 - uv1;

float f = 1.0f / (deltaUV1.x * deltaUV2.y - deltaUV2.x * deltaUV1.y);

glm::vec3 tangent;
tangent.x = f * (deltaUV2.y * edge1.x - deltaUV1.y * edge2.x);
tangent.y = f * (deltaUV2.y * edge1.y - deltaUV1.y * edge2.y);
tangent.z = f * (deltaUV2.y * edge1.z - deltaUV1.y * edge2.z);

glm::vec3 bitangent;
bitangent.x = f * (-deltaUV2.x * edge1.x + deltaUV1.x * edge2.x);
bitangent.y = f * (-deltaUV2.x * edge1.y + deltaUV1.x * edge2.y);
bitangent.z = f * (-deltaUV2.x * edge1.z + deltaUV1.x * edge2.z);

2. Accumulate Per-Vertex Vectors

When vertices are shared between triangles, add the calculated tangents and bitangents to each vertex's accumulator to achieve smooth shading across boundaries. After accumulating across all indices, normalize the accumulated vectors.

3. Orthogonalization via Gram-Schmidt

Due to mesh deformation, UV stretching, or vertex normal smoothing, \(T\), \(B\), and the surface normal \(N\) may not remain perpendicular. The Gram-Schmidt process re-orthogonalizes the tangent vector relative to \(N\):

\[T' = \text{normalize}(T - (T \cdot N) N)\]

To account for texture coordinate mirroring (handedness), the cross product determines orientation:

\[\text{handedness} = (\text{dot}(\text{cross}(N, T'), B) < 0.0) \; ? \; -1.0 : 1.0\] \[B' = \text{cross}(N, T') \times \text{handedness}\]

Constructing the TBN Matrix in GLSL

The tangent vector (and handedness flag, or explicit bitangent) is passed as a vertex attribute to OpenGL. The TBN matrix is constructed directly in the shader.

Vertex Shader

#version 330 core
layout (location = 0) in vec3 aPos;
layout (location = 1) in vec3 aNormal;
layout (location = 2) in vec2 aTexCoords;
layout (location = 3) in vec3 aTangent;

out vec2 TexCoords;
out mat3 TBN;

uniform mat4 model;
uniform mat4 view;
uniform mat4 projection;

void main()
{
    TexCoords = aTexCoords;
    
    mat3 normalMatrix = transpose(inverse(mat3(model)));
    vec3 T = normalize(normalMatrix * aTangent);
    vec3 N = normalize(normalMatrix * aNormal);
    
    // Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization
    T = normalize(T - dot(T, N) * N);
    vec3 B = cross(N, T);
    
    TBN = mat3(T, B, N);
    
    gl_Position = projection * view * model * vec4(aPos, 1.0);
}

Fragment Shader Application

The normal map sample is transformed from the \([0, 1]\) color range to the \([-1, 1]\) vector range and then multiplied by the TBN matrix:

#version 330 core
out vec4 FragColor;

in vec2 TexCoords;
in mat3 TBN;

uniform sampler2D normalMap;

void main()
{
    // Retrieve normal from normal map in range [0, 1]
    vec3 normal = texture(normalMap, TexCoords).rgb;
    // Remap to [-1, 1]
    normal = normal * 2.0 - 1.0;
    // Transform to world space
    normal = normalize(TBN * normal);

    // Apply lighting using 'normal'...
    FragColor = vec4(normal * 0.5 + 0.5, 1.0);
}

Alternatively, multiplying vectors by the transpose of the TBN matrix transforms light and view directions directly into tangent space inside the vertex shader, avoiding per-fragment matrix multiplication.
