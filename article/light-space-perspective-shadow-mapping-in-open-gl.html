Light Space Perspective Shadow Mapping in OpenGL

Light Space Perspective Shadow Mapping (LiSPSM) is an advanced shadowing technique designed to mitigate perspective aliasing by dynamically redistributing shadow map resolution. Standard shadow mapping wastes precision by uniformly allocating texels across the scene, causing visible pixelation near the viewer and redundant detail in the distance. LiSPSM resolves this in OpenGL by applying an additional perspective transformation in light space, effectively warping the depth buffer to prioritize regions close to the camera while maintaining light-space linearity and temporal stability.

The Problem: Perspective Aliasing

In traditional directional shadow mapping, the light uses an orthographic projection matrix. This creates a uniform grid of shadow texels across the scene. However, the eye camera observes the world through a perspective projection, where nearby objects occupy vastly more screen pixels than distant ones. When the uniform shadow map is projected onto screen space, the texel density near the camera is insufficient to match screen pixel resolution. This mismatch leads to severe perspective aliasing, manifesting as jagged, blocky shadow edges in the foreground.

The LiSPSM Perspective Warp

LiSPSM eliminates this discrepancy by applying a perspective projection directly inside light space before rasterizing the depth buffer. Instead of standard orthographic mapping, LiSPSM constructs a light projection matrix that behaves perspectively along an axis perpendicular to the light vector.

By bending coordinates toward the camera's view direction, the transformation assigns smaller light-space intervals to foreground geometry and larger intervals to the background. Consequently, foreground objects receive a significantly higher concentration of shadow map texels. Because the perspective transformation occurs purely in light space along the directional plane, it preserves the parallel nature of directional light rays while altering only the density distribution of depth samples.

Maintaining Stability with the Parameter \(n\)

Earlier warping approaches, such as Perspective Shadow Maps (PSMs), suffered from severe singularities and temporal flickering when the camera aligned with the light direction. LiSPSM solves this by introducing a configurable near-plane parameter, typically denoted as \(n\).

The parameter \(n\) dictates the strength of the perspective warp based on the angle \(\gamma\) between the view direction and the light direction:

  • Perpendicular Light (\(\gamma \approx 90^\circ\)): The maximum perspective warp is applied. Texel density is heavily biased toward the viewer's near plane, providing the greatest resolution boost where aliasing is most pronounced.
  • Parallel Light (\(\gamma \approx 0^\circ\) or \(180^\circ\)): Perspective warping offers diminishing returns and risks projection singularities. LiSPSM automatically increases \(n\), smoothly transitioning the projection back toward a standard uniform orthographic projection.

By dynamically scaling \(n\), LiSPSM guarantees that depth resolution never degenerates below standard uniform shadow mapping, preventing frame-to-frame popping.

OpenGL Implementation Pipeline

Integrating LiSPSM into an OpenGL pipeline requires constructing a customized light-projection matrix \(P_{LiSPSM}\):

  1. Calculate the Focus Region: Determine the intersection volume between the camera's view frustum and the scene's bounding volume. Any geometry outside this intersection that casts shadows into the frustum must also be included.
  2. Establish the Light-Space Coordinate Frame: Rotate the scene into light space, aligning the z-axis with the light direction. A secondary axis is oriented toward the camera's forward vector projected onto the plane perpendicular to the light.
  3. Compute the Perspective Matrix: Generate the LiSPSM projection matrix using the calculated parameter \(n\) and the bounding limits of the intersection volume.
  4. Depth Pass: Bind the shadow map framebuffer object (FBO) with an attached depth texture (GL_DEPTH_COMPONENT32F is recommended to minimize precision artifacts resulting from non-linear distribution). Multiply the light view matrix by \(P_{LiSPSM}\) and upload it to your vertex shader uniform.
  5. Lighting Pass: When sampling the shadow map during the forward or deferred lighting pass, transform the world-space fragment coordinates using the identical combined LiSPSM matrix. Use projective texture coordinates (textureProj in GLSL) to account for the perspective divide (\(w\)-component) introduced by the light-space warp.

Through this formulation, LiSPSM optimizes depth resolution utilization across the depth buffer, matching the observer's visual requirements without increasing the underlying texture dimensions or requiring multi-pass cascades.
