Fractional Even vs Integer Tessellation Spacing

In OpenGL, the tessellation primitive generation stage divides abstract patch edges into smaller segments based on tessellation levels and a designated spacing mode. The choice between integer spacing and fractional even spacing dictates whether the generated segments adapt continuously or abruptly when tessellation factors change dynamically. While integer spacing strictly produces equally sized segments using whole numbers, fractional even spacing introduces new geometry gradually, eliminating noticeable geometric popping during dynamic Level of Detail (LOD) transitions.

Integer Spacing (equal_spacing)

When an OpenGL tessellation evaluation shader uses equal_spacing, the floating-point tessellation level is clamped to the range \([1, \text{GL\_MAX\_TESS\_GEN\_LEVEL}]\) and rounded up to the nearest integer, \(n\).

The edge is then partitioned into exactly \(n\) segments of identical length, where each segment length is \(1/n\) of the total parametric edge.

Because the segment count jumps directly from one whole integer to the next, any continuous modification of the tessellation level—such as moving the camera toward an object—causes sudden changes in mesh density. When the level increases past an integer boundary (e.g., from 3.0 to 3.01), a new vertex instantly appears, and the lengths of all existing segments contract instantaneously. This produces visible "popping" artifacts in rendered geometry.

Fractional Even Spacing (fractional_even_spacing)

To resolve geometric popping, fractional_even_spacing provides continuous, smooth transitions. The incoming tessellation level is clamped to \([2, \text{GL\_MAX\_TESS\_GEN\_LEVEL}]\) and rounded up to the next even integer, \(n\).

Unlike integer spacing, the generated segments are not all required to be identical in length during intermediate steps. Instead, the edge is divided into \(n - 2\) segments of equal length and two additional, symmetric segments that scale dynamically:

  • When the tessellation level exactly equals an even integer \(n - 2\), the two additional segments have a length of zero, effectively rendering \(n - 2\) equal segments.
  • As the tessellation level continuously increases toward \(n\), the two scaling segments gradually expand from zero length until all \(n\) segments reach equal lengths.
  • This smooth transition ensures that new vertices emerge continuously from the boundary instead of snapping into place.

Because the maximum division count is always an even number, symmetry is preserved across the center of the patch edge.

Summary of Differences

  1. Minimum Tessellation Factor: Integer spacing allows a minimum factor of 1 (a single undivided edge), whereas fractional even spacing clamps the minimum factor to 2.
  2. Segment Uniformity: Integer spacing guarantees that every segment along an edge is identical in length at all times. Fractional even spacing produces segments of non-uniform length while the tessellation factor falls between even integers.
  3. Visual Continuity: Integer spacing causes sudden popping artifacts when tessellation factors fluctuate dynamically. Fractional even spacing allows seamless geometric morphing, making it significantly better suited for smooth distance-based LOD implementations.
