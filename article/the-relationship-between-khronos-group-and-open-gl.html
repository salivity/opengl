The Relationship Between Khronos Group and OpenGL

The Khronos Group serves as the governing consortium responsible for defining, maintaining, and certifying the OpenGL application programming interface (API). Originally developed by Silicon Graphics Inc. (SGI), the governance of OpenGL was transferred to Khronos in 2006 to establish an open, royalty-free, and vendor-neutral graphics standard. While Khronos has largely shifted its primary development focus toward modern low-level APIs such as Vulkan, the consortium continues to oversee the OpenGL specification to guarantee cross-platform compatibility, hardware interoperability, and long-term ecosystem stability.

The Role of Khronos as Governing Body

The Khronos Group is a non-profit, member-funded industry consortium consisting of major hardware vendors, software developers, and platform creators, including companies like NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Google, and Apple. Within Khronos, the OpenGL Working Group is tasked with drafting and refining the official OpenGL specifications. Rather than writing the software drivers directly—a task left to individual graphics hardware vendors—Khronos defines the precise mathematical and behavioral rules that those drivers must follow.

The Specification Process and Extensions

The ongoing specification of OpenGL relies heavily on an extension mechanism overseen by Khronos. When a hardware manufacturer develops new graphical capabilities, it introduces extensions categorized by their level of consensus:

  • Vendor-Specific Extensions: Created by a single vendor (e.g., NV, AMD) for proprietary features.
  • EXT Extensions: Collaborative features supported by two or more hardware vendors.
  • ARB Extensions: Approved by the Architecture Review Board working group within Khronos, signifying high standardization.

If an extension proves broadly useful across multiple platforms and architectures, the Khronos working group incorporates it directly into the next core OpenGL specification release.

Conformance and Certification

To ensure that the OpenGL specification is executed consistently across different operating systems and graphics processing units (GPUs), Khronos maintains the OpenGL Conformance Test Suite (CTS). Hardware vendors must submit their implementation results to Khronos and pass these rigorous tests to legally use the OpenGL trademark and claim compliance. This verification process prevents fragmentation across the industry.

Current State of the Specification

OpenGL is currently considered a mature and stable API. The last major update was OpenGL 4.6, released by Khronos in 2017, which notably integrated support for SPIR-V intermediate shaders, aligning OpenGL's shading pipeline with Vulkan.

Today, the ongoing specification work for OpenGL focuses on maintenance, defect correction, and specialized extensions rather than aggressive core feature expansion. Modern graphics development emphasizes explicit control and multi-threading capabilities found in Khronos's newer API, Vulkan. Nonetheless, Khronos remains committed to preserving OpenGL's backward compatibility, updating its registry, and ensuring that legacy graphics infrastructure remains reliable for enterprise, academic, and consumer applications globally.
