Barycentric Coordinates in OpenGL Rasterization

During the rasterization stage of modern OpenGL, barycentric coordinates serve as the mathematical foundation for converting geometric primitives into discrete screen fragments. They primarily function to determine whether a given pixel sample lies inside a triangle and to perform perspective-correct interpolation of vertex attributes—such as texture coordinates, normals, and colors—across the surface of the primitive before passing this data to the fragment shader.

Inside-Primitive Testing (Coverage Determination)

In modern GPU hardware, the rasterizer takes the screen-space coordinates of a primitive's vertices to set up edge functions. Barycentric coordinates express any point \(P\) inside a triangle defined by vertices \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\) as a weighted sum:

\[P = \lambda_A A + \lambda_B B + \lambda_C C\]

where \(\lambda_A + \lambda_B + \lambda_C = 1\).

The fixed-function rasterizer calculates these scalar weights (\(\lambda_A, \lambda_B, \lambda_C\)) for pixel sample centers. If all three weights are greater than or equal to zero, the sample point is inside the triangle boundaries. This test dictates which fragment threads the GPU must instantiate for execution.

Perspective-Correct Attribute Interpolation

Primitives undergo perspective projection, which introduces a non-linear relationship between screen-space coordinates and 3D view-space distances. Linearly interpolating vertex attributes directly in 2D screen space causes severe texture warping and visual artifacts.

Barycentric coordinates solve this via perspective-correct interpolation. The rasterizer divides vertex attributes and barycentric weights by the homogeneous clip-space depth coordinate (\(w\)). The interpolated attribute \(I\) at a fragment is evaluated as:

\[I = \frac{\frac{\lambda_A \cdot I_A}{w_A} + \frac{\lambda_B \cdot I_B}{w_B} + \frac{\lambda_C \cdot I_C}{w_C}}{\frac{\lambda_A}{w_A} + \frac{\lambda_B}{w_B} + \frac{\lambda_C}{w_C}}\]

This guarantees that data passed to in variables in the fragment shader accurately represents the 3D position and orientation of the surface.

Fragment Quad Derivatives and Texture Filtering

Rasterization hardware evaluates fragments in \(2 \times 2\) pixel grids. The rate of change of barycentric coordinates across these adjacent fragments allows the GPU to compute screen-space partial derivatives (dFdx and dFdy). These derivatives are required for calculating level-of-detail (LOD) values during texture mapping, preventing aliasing via automatic mipmap selection.

Explicit Access in Modern Pipelines

While barycentric weights have historically operated solely within fixed-function hardware, modern extensions (such as GL_NV_fragment_shader_barycentric and GL_AMD_shader_explicit_vertex_parameter) expose these raw coordinates directly to the fragment shader. This enables single-pass wireframe rendering, custom analytic antialiasing, and procedural geometry modification without requiring extra geometry shader stages or duplicating vertex data.
