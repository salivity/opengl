SPH Boundary Conditions in OpenGL Shaders

Simulating Smoothed-Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) in OpenGL compute shaders requires robust boundary condition handling to prevent particle tunneling, maintain numerical stability, and enforce realistic fluid behavior. Because shaders update particle states concurrently at discrete time steps, boundary enforcement must explicitly resolve spatial limits, velocity responses, and neighbor-support deficiencies. This guide outlines the mandatory boundary checks required within the integration and pressure-solve passes of an OpenGL SPH pipeline.

Domain Extent Clamping (AABB Checks)

The most fundamental check confines particles within the simulation's Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB). At the end of the numerical integration step (such as Euler or Verlet integration), the shader must evaluate whether the updated position vector exceeds the minimum or maximum domain bounds:

  • Position Projection: If position.xyz exceeds boxMax.xyz or falls below boxMin.xyz, the position must be clamped to the boundary minus an epsilon offset to prevent out-of-bounds indexing in spatial hash grids.
  • Sign Inversion: The corresponding velocity component along the breached axis must be reversed.

Surface Penetration and Normal Resolution

For arbitrary boundaries or complex static geometry, standard box checks are insufficient. The shader must evaluate signed distance functions (SDFs) or plane equations to detect penetration:

  • Distance Evaluation: The shader must compute the signed distance \(d = \mathbf{n} \cdot (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p})\) where \(\mathbf{n}\) is the boundary normal and \(\mathbf{p}\) is a point on the surface. If \(d < r\) (where \(r\) is the particle smoothing radius), collision resolution triggers.
  • Contact Projection: The particle’s position must be explicitly repositioned outside the boundary along the normal vector: \(\mathbf{x}_{\text{corrected}} = \mathbf{x} + (\text{penetration\_depth}) \mathbf{n}\).

Velocity Dampening and Friction

Inverting velocity without energy dissipation quickly destabilizes an SPH simulation. A mandatory post-collision velocity update decomposes the particle's velocity vector \(\mathbf{v}\) into normal (\(\mathbf{v}_n\)) and tangential (\(\mathbf{v}_t\)) components:

  • Normal Restitution: The normal velocity must be scaled by a coefficient of restitution \(c_r \in [0, 1]\): \(\mathbf{v}_n' = -c_r \mathbf{v}_n\).
  • Tangential Friction: To simulate boundary friction (no-slip or free-slip conditions), the tangential velocity must be attenuated using a friction coefficient \(\mu\): \(\mathbf{v}_t' = (1 - \mu) \mathbf{v}_t\).
  • Final Assembly: The shader writes the recombined velocity \(\mathbf{v}' = \mathbf{v}_n' + \mathbf{v}_t'\) back to the buffer.

Tunneling Prevention (CFL Condition Guard)

High-velocity particles can pass entirely through boundary volumes within a single shader dispatch if their displacement per step exceeds the boundary thickness or the SPH smoothing radius \(h\).

  • Maximum Displacement Check: The shader must verify that \(\|\mathbf{v}\| \Delta t \le \alpha h\), where \(\alpha\) is typically between \(0.1\) and \(0.4\) according to the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) condition.
  • Ray-Casting Fallback: If adaptive sub-stepping is omitted to maintain frame rates, the shader must perform continuous collision detection (CCD) via a ray-cast from the previous position \(\mathbf{x}_{t}\) to the candidate position \(\mathbf{x}_{t+\Delta t}\), halting the particle at the first intersection point.

Boundary Deficiency and Repulsion Forces

Because SPH relies on a symmetric kernel support domain, particles near a wall lack fluid neighbors on the exterior side. This truncation leads to severe particle clustering along boundaries (particle sticking) due to erroneous pressure calculations. To offset this, shaders must implement one of the following mandatory checks during the force-computation pass:

  • Repulsive Boundary Potential: Apply a repulsive force, such as the Monaghan-Kajtar penalty force, whenever the distance \(d\) to a boundary falls below the smoothing length \(h\). The force magnitude scales inversely with distance to counteract fluid pressure driving particles into the wall.
  • Frozen/Dummy Particle Contribution: If using boundary particles stored in a buffer, the compute shader must include these static particles in the standard density summation and pressure force loops, setting their velocity to zero or computing an artificial mirrored velocity to correctly satisfy the no-slip condition.
