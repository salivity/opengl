OpenGL Depth Buffer Z-Coordinate Mapping Function

This article explains the mathematical functions OpenGL uses to map view-space z-coordinates into the depth buffer. It covers the core transformation steps, including the perspective projection matrix, the perspective divide to normalized device coordinates, and the final viewport depth-range mapping, while detailing why the resulting depth representation is non-linear.

In OpenGL, the mapping of camera-space coordinates to the depth buffer depends on whether perspective or orthographic projection is used. In standard perspective projection, the transformation from view space (eye space) to depth buffer space is non-linear and governed by a reciprocal function proportional to \(1/z\).

The transformation process occurs in three main stages:

First, the standard OpenGL perspective projection matrix converts the view-space position \((x_e, y_e, z_e, 1)\) into clip space \((x_c, y_c, z_c, w_c)\). Given a near clipping plane \(n\) and a far clipping plane \(f\) (where both \(n\) and \(f\) are positive distances), the clip-space depth coordinate \(z_c\) and clip-space homogeneous coordinate \(w_c\) are calculated as:

\[z_c = -\frac{f + n}{f - n} z_e - \frac{2fn}{f - n}\] \[w_c = -z_e\]

Because the camera looks down the negative z-axis in OpenGL eye space, \(z_e\) is negative for visible geometry, making \(w_c\) a positive value representing positive eye-space distance \(d = -z_e\).

Second, the hardware executes the perspective divide to generate Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC). Dividing \(z_c\) by \(w_c\) yields:

\[z_{ndc} = \frac{z_c}{w_c} = \frac{\frac{f+n}{f-n} d - \frac{2fn}{f-n}}{d} = \frac{f + n}{f - n} - \frac{2fn}{f - n} \cdot \frac{1}{d}\]

This operation constrains \(z_{ndc}\) to the range \([-1, 1]\), where the near plane maps to \(-1\) and the far plane maps to \(1\).

Third, OpenGL applies the viewport depth transformation (glDepthRange), which maps the \([-1, 1]\) NDC interval into the window-space depth buffer range \([0, 1]\) (assuming default parameters):

\[z_w = \frac{z_{ndc} + 1}{2}\]

Substituting the expression for \(z_{ndc}\) into the window-space equation yields the direct function mapping eye distance \(d\) to the depth value \(z_w\):

\[z_w(d) = \frac{f}{f - n} - \frac{f \cdot n}{f - n} \cdot \frac{1}{d}\]

This equation demonstrates that depth values are distributed inversely proportional to eye distance (\(1/d\)). As a result, the vast majority of depth buffer precision is allocated near the near plane. For example, half of the available depth values typically represent distances within a very short distance of \(n\).

In contrast, an orthographic projection uses a purely linear mapping function:

\[z_w(d) = \frac{d - n}{f - n}\]

Under orthographic projection, \(w_c = 1\), bypassing the reciprocal division and distributing floating-point or integer depth precision evenly across the entire viewing volume.
