OpenGL GL_RASTERIZER_DISCARD Explained

The GL_RASTERIZER_DISCARD capability in OpenGL is an optimization flag that disables the primitive rasterization stage of the graphics pipeline. When enabled, the GPU continues to execute vertex, tessellation, and geometry shaders, but discards primitives before they can generate fragments. This mechanism is primarily utilized when the pipeline is repurposed for non-rendering tasks—such as vertex-based compute workloads and Transform Feedback operations—allowing developers to extract processed data without incurring the performance costs of fragment shading and framebuffer operations.

Mechanics of GL_RASTERIZER_DISCARD

In standard graphics execution, primitives processed by the geometry or vertex stages are sent to the rasterizer, which interpolates attributes across primitive surfaces and generates fragments for the fragment shader.

When you enable the discard flag using:

glEnable(GL_RASTERIZER_DISCARD);

OpenGL halts primitive flow immediately after the last active pre-rasterization shader stage (Vertex, Tessellation, or Geometry). The rasterizer is bypassed entirely. Consequently:

  • No fragments are generated.
  • The fragment shader does not execute.
  • Per-fragment operations, including depth, stencil, blending, and multisampling tests, are skipped.
  • No data is written to the bound framebuffer or color attachments.

To resume normal rendering, you disable the flag:

glDisable(GL_RASTERIZER_DISCARD);

Role in Transform Feedback and Pre-Rasterization Compute

While dedicated compute shaders (GL_COMPUTE_SHADER) bypass the graphics pipeline inherently without needing this flag, OpenGL pipelines often use standard shader stages for computational tasks. The most prominent example is Transform Feedback, where the GPU calculates physics, particle movement, or procedural geometry inside vertex or geometry shaders and writes the results back into a buffer object.

In such workflows, rendering pixels to a screen is unnecessary. Using GL_RASTERIZER_DISCARD ensures that:

  1. Memory Bandwidth is Conserved: The GPU avoids reading from or writing to color, depth, and stencil buffers.
  2. ALU Cycles are Saved: Heavy fragment shading passes are not invoked for intermediate computational data.
  3. Execution Time is Minimized: The GPU strictly focuses execution resources on vertex processing, buffer updates, and Transform Feedback streams.

Impact on Dedicated Compute Shaders

Dedicated compute shaders invoked via glDispatchCompute do not traverse the primitive pipeline; they execute in a standalone stage independent of draw calls. Therefore, GL_RASTERIZER_DISCARD has no direct effect on native compute shader dispatches.

However, in hybrid architectures where compute tasks are distributed across vertex manipulation and Transform Feedback stages (often to maintain compatibility with older hardware or specific rendering paths), GL_RASTERIZER_DISCARD acts as the critical switch that converts a standard rendering pipeline into a dedicated geometry-processing compute unit.
