PN-Triangles in the OpenGL Tessellation Pipeline

This article explains how Point-Normal Triangles (PN-Triangles) are generated within OpenGL's hardware tessellation pipeline. By converting flat triangular meshes into smooth, curved surface patches on the GPU, PN-Triangles dramatically increase geometric fidelity without requiring changes to the underlying topology or heavy CPU preprocessing. The following sections break down the end-to-end implementation across the Vertex Shader, Tessellation Control Shader, Primitive Generator, and Tessellation Evaluation Shader.

Overview of PN-Triangles

A PN-Triangle replaces each flat triangle of a mesh with a curved triangular Bézier patch. The patch uses a cubic Bézier polynomial (defined by 10 control points) to compute surface positions and a quadratic polynomial (defined by 6 control points) to compute interpolated normals. The control points are derived entirely on the fly from the triangle’s original three vertices and their associated per-vertex normal vectors.

1. Vertex Shader Stage

In a PN-Triangle implementation, the Vertex Shader performs minimal work. Instead of transforming vertices directly into clip space via the model-view-projection matrix, it typically outputs vertex positions and normals in object space or world space. This preserves the geometric orientation necessary for accurate control point computation in the next stage.

2. Tessellation Control Shader (TCS)

The TCS is responsible for two primary operations: setting the level of detail and calculating the control net for the Bézier patch.

  • Tessellation Levels: The TCS determines gl_TessLevelOuter (for the three edges) and gl_TessLevelInner (for the patch interior), either statically or dynamically based on metrics such as camera distance or screen-space edge length.
  • Position Control Points (\(b_{ijk}\)): The cubic patch requires 10 control points. The corner points (\(b_{300}, b_{030}, b_{003}\)) are the original vertices. The two edge control points per edge (\(b_{210}, b_{120}\), etc.) are calculated by projecting the midpoint displacement onto the tangent plane defined by the vertex normals. The center control point (\(b_{111}\)) is constructed from the average of the edge points and the original vertices to prevent the center from sagging or pinching.
  • Normal Control Points (\(n_{ijk}\)): The quadratic normal field requires 6 control points. The corner normals (\(n_{200}, n_{020}, n_{002}\)) are the original vertex normals. The intermediate edge normals (\(n_{110}, n_{011}, n_{101}\)) are derived by reflecting the sum of the vertex normals across the plane perpendicular to the edge direction.

These calculated points are output through custom patch output variables to the downstream stages.

3. Tessellation Primitive Generator (Fixed-Function)

Once the TCS finishes executing for a patch, the fixed-function primitive generator subdivides the abstract triangular domain according to the specified tessellation levels. It produces a dense mesh of micro-triangles characterized by barycentric coordinates \((u, v, w)\), where \(u + v + w = 1\). No geometry has been curved yet; this stage purely outputs domain coordinates.

4. Tessellation Evaluation Shader (TES)

The TES runs once for every barycentric coordinate \((u, v, w)\) generated by the primitive generator. It evaluates the PN-Triangle equations:

  • Position Evaluation: The TES evaluates the cubic triangular Bernstein polynomial using the 10 position control points received from the TCS: \[P(u, v, w) = \sum_{i+j+k=3} \frac{3!}{i!j!k!} u^i v^j w^k b_{ijk}\]
  • Normal Evaluation: It evaluates the quadratic polynomial using the 6 normal control points: \[N(u, v, w) = \sum_{i+j+k=2} \frac{2!}{i!j!k!} u^i v^j w^k n_{ijk}\] The resulting normal vector is normalized to correct for non-linear interpolation artifacts.
  • Transform: Finally, the evaluated surface position \(P(u, v, w)\) is transformed by the Model-View-Projection matrix and written to gl_Position. The evaluated normal is passed to the Fragment Shader for per-pixel lighting.
